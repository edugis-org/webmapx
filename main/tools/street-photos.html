Street photos

Shows a street-level photo of a spot on the map.

type: street-photostoolbarstable

What it does

Street photos answers "what does it look like there?". Click a spot and the panel shows a photo taken at street level, looking at that spot.

The photos come from one of two services:

  • Panoramax, the default. An open, federated collection of street photos. It needs no account or API key, the photos are openly licensed, and a click goes to an open project rather than an advertising company. Coverage depends on what people have uploaded, so some places have none.
  • Google Street View, offered only when the config has a Google API key. Panoramax stays the default, because every Google request sends the clicked position to Google.

While the tool is open with Panoramax chosen, the map shows where photos exist: lines along the photographed routes, and one dot per photo at street level. This is a layer called "Panoramax photos". It is listed in the legend while the tool is open, goes away when the tool closes, and is not stored in permalinks.

Google allows its coverage layer only through its own map library, so with Google chosen there is no coverage map.

Using it

  1. Open Street photos from the toolbar.
  2. Zoom in until the photographed routes appear. Further out than zoom level 6 there is nothing to show, and a click does not search.
  3. Click a route, or click a photo dot to see exactly that photo. A click anywhere else looks for the nearest photo facing that spot.
  4. Use Colour by to color the routes by coverage, by date, or by camera.
  5. Choose Open larger picture to enlarge it, or Open 360 view for a 360° photo, which you can turn by dragging or with the arrow keys.
  6. In the enlarged view, Open in Panoramax or Open in Google Street View takes you to that service. Escape closes the enlarged view first and the panel only on the next press.

If the config has a Google key, Photos from switches between the services.

In your own map

Add street-photos to a toolbar. Panoramax then works with no further configuration. The older name panoramax is still accepted.

To offer Google Street View, give the tool a key:

"tools": {
  "street-photos": { "googleApiKey": "{key-google}" }
}

The key needs the Street View Static API enabled and a billing account. It is visible in every visitor's browser, so restrict it to that API and to your own sites. A click first asks Google whether there is imagery, which is free; only then are images loaded, which Google bills per image.

This key is separate from the info tool's googleApiKey, which puts Street View in the info panel.

Other options are searchDistance (metres, default 50), and apiUrl and viewerUrl, which point the tool at one Panoramax instance instead of all of them.

The coverage map needs vector tiles, which MapLibre and OpenLayers draw. On Leaflet and Cesium the panel says the map cannot show coverage, and clicking still works.

Extending it

utils/street-photo.ts is the one photo model both services produce, so the panel does not know which service answered. utils/panoramax.ts and utils/google-street-view.ts are pure functions for URLs, parsing and colors.

The search uses Panoramax's place_position, which finds pictures looking at a spot rather than pictures merely taken near it. How far it looks follows the zoom: about 12 pixels on screen, between searchDistance and 500 m.

The 360° viewer is Pannellum. It is loaded the first time someone enlarges a 360° photo, so a visitor who never does pays nothing for it.

Panoramax's density grid is left out on purpose: it draws an area with one photo as large as one with thousands.
